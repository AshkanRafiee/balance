Money your bank accounted for but your phone never received, movements dated by the bank's own clock, and the reason and the channel behind a movement read straight from the bank's message.

• Unaccounted money: when the balance a bank states changes by more than the messages that reached the app account for, the difference now appears as an amber "Unaccounted" row on the later statement's date and is counted in every total. It is arithmetic on two balance statements the bank itself sent, so the figure is proven — the app never invents the missing movements, it does not say how many there were, what they were for or when they happened, and it says so on the row. It is exported with the history, marked apart from the real movements, and nothing before the first statement or after the last one can be detected.
• Movements are now dated by the time the bank wrote in the message, not by the day the SMS arrived, so a message that turns up weeks late lands in the period it really belongs to. A stated time is believed only over the span in which a message can genuinely travel late — outside that, the arrival still stands, and a stated date only closes a gap when the bank's own figures back it up.
• Each bank's dates are read in the calendar that bank declares, so a message from a Gregorian bank no longer files a movement in the wrong month, and an impossible date is no longer quietly rolled into a day the bank never mentioned.
• Movement reasons read from the bank: where a bank's message names the event behind a movement — a phone top-up, a bill payment, a refund, an instant transfer — the history says so on the row, in your own language, without you having to type it. Where the bank states no reason, and for plain deposits and withdrawals that only repeat the direction, nothing extra is shown, and a reason is never mixed into your own note. Reasons are kept in a store of their own, travel through encrypted backups and appear in CSV exports.
• Movement channels read from the bank: when an SMS says how a transaction went through — Shetab, the instant payment system, a point-of-sale terminal, mobile banking or a bank branch — the history shows it on its own quiet line under the row, in your language, and it follows the movement through backups and exports.
• The history now opens collapsed, so even a very long account appears in a moment: open a year for its months and a month for its days, and each level brings the one below it. Movements load in batches as you reach them instead of all at once, so scrolling stays smooth, the screen shows the shape of the history while it loads rather than a blank page, and the heading says how many movements are on screen. The Display menu's "History: expand all" switch is gone with the cascade it used to ask for.
• Revoking SMS access no longer blanks the dashboard: the balances already read stay on screen, an amber strip under the total card says they may be out of date, and tapping it asks for access again (or opens the app's settings page when the denial is permanent). A scan in progress no longer overwrites a theme, language or currency change made while it was running, and going back to "follow the system" language now really does follow the system.
• The home-screen widget follows a color theme of its own, set by a new "Widget theme" selector in the Display menu and defaulting to the app's, so a dark app with a light widget on a light wallpaper finally looks the way you asked for.
• Smaller fixes: tapping the account number in a single-account history copies it and clears it from the clipboard again after the paste window, a long account number no longer pushes the export button off the bar, a balance statement that arrives late no longer overwrites a newer balance, and a screen reader now reads a row's reason, channel and your own note — a row that already has a note no longer invites you to add one.
• A message the rules cannot read no longer costs the scan seconds: one-line bank messages took so long to give up that they held up the balance scan and every backup, and are now microseconds. The bank message samples in the public repository no longer carry a real first name, real balances, real amounts, a real merchant or a real one-time code — every figure in them is invented and the fixture header says so.
